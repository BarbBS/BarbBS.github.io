Liquidity is not observed directly. We compare low-frequency liquidity proxies built from daily prices with high-frequency benchmarks and ask which proxies work, when they fail and how liquidity co-moves with volatility.

## Main questions

Which daily proxy best tracks unobserved liquidity, especially in periods of extreme illiquidity? Are proxies that capture different dimensions of liquidity – price impact, depth, width, resiliency – coherent with each other? How does liquidity react to price jumps, zero returns and aggressive orders? Is there commonality in liquidity across stocks and markets?

## Methods

Intraday benchmarks of spreads and price impact, copulas and tail dependence, extreme value theory, causality tests and mixed-frequency (MIDAS) models, applied to the Warsaw Stock Exchange, other European markets and cryptocurrency markets.

## Key findings

Under extreme illiquidity, the Closing Quoted Spread based on daily bid and ask prices is the best low-frequency proxy; it shows the highest tail dependence with intraday percent spreads.

Around intraday zero returns, liquidity measures from different dimensions behave incoherently: transaction costs fall, but trading activity drops, and the effects are short-lived.
